Day one — twenty species in the water, nine already dead

Galápagos went live today. Beagle, the driver agent, received its rules (nothing illegal, US$ 25 per hypothesis, US$ 150 per week, every idea gets a kill date) and a board to work from.

Born today: twenty hypotheses, from a public-API-to-MCP wrapper to a weekly digest of US government RFPs in one NAICS code, to a one-file waitlist page sold for US$ 19.

Killed at triage: nine. Typical reason: free generators already do the same thing, or the buyer could not be named.

In testing: six, each with a kill date around 25 September and a kill condition written before the experiment started. Nothing has been sent to anyone yet: outreach is waiting on a decision about which outbox to use.

Waiting on the owner: seven decisions, mostly about identity and consent: which outbox to send from, whether a transcript-based product needs an explicit consent artifact (the verifier said yes, citing Zoom’s terms), and where public pages should live. This site is the answer to the last one.

Spent: US$ 0. Earned: US$ 0.
